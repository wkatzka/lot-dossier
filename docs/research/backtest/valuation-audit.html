2020: transactions explicitly marked LAND, filtered for repeat, deed and multi-parcel issues; historical acreage coverage was inadequate. 2024: sale-year vacant-land classification corroborated by prior-year vacant classification and zero building assessment. Blank transaction-type fields are allowed in this second cohort and remain a classification limitation. These are different cohorts, not an apples-to-apples improvement comparison. The two 2024 rows use the same test sales. The ±$50k policy chooses by 2022 hit rate only; it was added after seeing the initial MAE result and is a policy sensitivity, not a second independent validation.

Each model uses nonlinear gradient-boosted hedonic estimation. The 2024 model uses latitude, longitude, marketYear, priorAssessedLandLog, grossAreaLog, floodZone, walkScore, township. Assessments are historical predictors; targets are actual county-reported sale prices. Area comes from WGS84 geodesic measurement of 2020 parcel polygons, not current parcel size. Missing and conflicting geometry stays unknown.

5. Validation controls

  • 2024 partitions: 742 training (2021), 846 tuning (2022), 697 calibration (2023), 613 untouched test targets (2024).
  • Repeated parcel identities are embargoed across partitions. Hyperparameters are chosen on tuning-period ±$50k hit rate (dollar error breaks ties); transforms and feature inclusion use training data only.
  • The 2024 holdout was evaluated after the first 2020 run identified historical area coverage problems. No 2024 outcomes were used to select features or hyperparameters; the separately reported tolerance-objective sensitivity was added afterward.
  • Point predictions do not require enough observations to calibrate intervals. The nominal 90% intervals covered 94.5% of 2024 sales, but had median width $1,688,410; wide coverage does not satisfy ±$50k.
  • No holdout records were deleted because their errors were large. Prices are not winsorized or replaced by estimates.
  • These are reconstructed historical tables retrieved today. Later source corrections and unknown original publication times prevent a strict point-in-time public-information claim.
